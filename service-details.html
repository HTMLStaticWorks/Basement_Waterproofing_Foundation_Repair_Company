Foundation Crack Repair & Wall Stabilization

High-pressure structural epoxy injection and carbon fiber reinforcement grid technology to stop subterranean seepage and restore concrete load capacity.

Understanding Foundation Cracks

Poured concrete and masonry block foundation walls are subjected to intense hydro-static water pressure and soil expansion-contraction cycles. When soil moisture swells against foundation walls, structural tension creates cracks. If left unaddressed, water continuously trickles into the basement, deteriorating interior drywall, framing, and indoor air quality.

Vertical Cracks
Commonly caused by concrete shrinkage during curing. High water seepage risk.
Horizontal Cracks
Caused by heavy lateral soil pressure. High structural bowing danger.
Stair-Step Cracks
Follow mortar joints in block walls due to differential foundation settlement.

The FoundationGuard Dual-Action System

Unlike temporary hydraulic cement patches applied to the wall surface, FoundationGuard utilizes high-pressure structural resin injection combined with aerospace-grade carbon fiber straps.

1. High-Pressure Resin Injection

Liquid epoxy or expansive polyurethane resin is injected under 1,500+ PSI through mechanical ports placed every 6 inches along the fissure. The resin fills the crack completely from interior to exterior ground face, bonding the concrete with up to 10,000 PSI tensile strength.

2. Carbon Fiber Reinforcement Grid

Woven carbon fiber straps are bonded vertically over the repaired crack using structural epoxy adhesive. Carbon fiber is 10x stronger than steel, ensuring zero future inward wall deflection.

Our 4-Step Crack Repair Methodology

STEP 1
Surface Prep & Injection Port Placement

The concrete fissure is cleaned of loose debris. Injection ports are installed along the crack line at precise intervals.

STEP 2
Surface Seal Cap Application

Fast-curing epoxy capping paste is applied over the exterior face of the crack to contain injected resin under pressure.

STEP 3
High-Pressure Polymer Injection

Liquid resin is systematically pumped from bottom port to top until liquid bleeds out the adjacent port, confirming complete penetration.

STEP 4
Carbon Fiber Strap Bonding

High-tensile carbon fiber grid straps are saturated in epoxy resin and anchored into top floor joists and sub-slab concrete.
